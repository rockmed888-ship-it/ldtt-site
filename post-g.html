An AI can only misuse the tools you hand it. A stamp says which ones it got.

A free Observe stamp that shows this connector was checked — so you can run it with clearer eyes.

More people are wiring AI assistants to drone tools: ask a question, the AI calls a connector, the connector talks to the aircraft or its data. That's handy. It also means the AI can reach whatever that connector can reach.

Security groups already list "tool misuse" and "too much access" among the top risks for AI agents. The fix starts with a plain question:

What can this connector actually do if an AI calls it?

What an LDTT stamp tells you

  • A level, in plain words. Observe means read telemetry only. No motion commands, no parameter writes, no shell. An AI using an Observe connector can look, not fly.
  • A declared send list, checked against what that level allows.
  • One exact build, named by version and a digest you can recompute.
  • Signed and revocable, so a bad build can be switched off.

Big AI certification programs check whole companies or whole agents, and they cost real money. LDTT is smaller and free: one connector, one build, one level you can check yourself.

LDTT is about the connector, not the flight. It isn't a flight clearance and makes no regulatory claims.
