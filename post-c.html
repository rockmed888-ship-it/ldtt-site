Our first trust stamp lists its own weak spots

A free Observe stamp that shows this connector was checked — so you can run it with clearer eyes.

A trust stamp should tell you what passed and what isn't proven yet. Ours does both, in the file itself.

Linked Drone Tool Trust (LDTT) has published its first Observe stamp. It covers our own reference connector, a small tool that only reads drone telemetry.

What the stamp says

  • Level: Observe. It reads telemetry only, with no motion commands in scope and no internet egress.
  • Build-bound. It names one connector, one version, and one build digest. The build is reproducible and was rebuilt in CI with a matching digest.
  • Provenance attached. GitHub SLSA v1 provenance comes from our public CI workflow.
  • Signed in public. The stamp is keyless-signed by our public GitHub workflow and logged in a public transparency log.
  • Revocable. A signed revoke list that refreshes automatically can switch it off.

What the stamp admits

  • There's no hosted release asset yet. You get the build from the repo, not a download page.
  • MAVLink message signing is off for this connector. That's allowed at Observe, and the stamp says so.

Checked, declared, revocable, and honest about the gaps.

Why put weak spots in the stamp?

The person about to run a drone connector deserves the whole picture, not just the green parts. Our first version of this stamp said the build was local. When CI closed that gap, we re-signed the stamp and updated the note. Every gap we close works the same way.

LDTT covers the connector, not the flight. It isn't a flight clearance, it isn't required to fly, and it makes no regulatory claims.
